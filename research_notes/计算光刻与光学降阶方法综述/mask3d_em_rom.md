## Q1. Domain decomposition (DDM), edge/corner libraries and boundary-layer models: how near-field corrections are added to the Kirchhoff mask

### Takeaway
The production-grade route from rigorous EM to full-chip OPC for DUV and EUV has been **edge-based DDM**. Rigorously pre-computed edge (and later corner and edge-to-edge coupling) near-field "signals" are superposed onto the Kirchhoff/thin-mask transmission. A close relative is the **boundary-layer model (BLM)**: sub-resolution strips of fixed width and complex transmission placed along every edge. Both are linear-superposition corrections. Their main known limit is nonlinear crosstalk between closely spaced edges, which later vendor work handles with coupling terms, kernel filters and, for EUV, angle-dependent (azimuth-interpolated or hybrid Hopkins-Abbe) signals.

### Cited Findings
**Foundations (pre-2010)**

- Core idea of DDM: the near field of a complex mask is built up from the diffraction of simpler component masks, such as isolated edges. Stated limit: edge-DDM cannot capture the nonlinear crosstalk between nearby edges, because each edge is treated in isolation — [US patent family, Three-dimensional mask model](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9372957)
- Tirapu-Azpiroz, Burchard & Yablonovitch, "Boundary layer model to account for thick mask effects in photolithography," Proc. SPIE 5040 (2003), DOI 10.1117/12.488803. Described as "a sophisticated variation on the Kirchhoff approximation capable of modeling rigorous near field effects while retaining the simplicity of the scalar model" — [SPIE record](https://proceedings.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/5040/0000/Boundary-layer-model-to-account-for-thick-mask-effects-in/10.1117/12.488803.full)
- Tirapu-Azpiroz & Yablonovitch, "Incorporating mask topography edge diffraction in photolithography simulations," JOSA A 23(4), 821–828 (2006). Extends the BLM to alternating PSMs with local rectangular perturbations of adjustable width, transmission and phase near phase edges. In one comparison the aerial-image error was about 3.3% for the BLM against about 57% for the thin-mask approximation — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7174736_Incorporating_mask_topography_edge_diffraction_in_photolithography_simulations)
- BLM mechanics as described in later patents: edge fields are rendered as "small strip-like features of essentially fixed transmission" along aperture boundaries. The strips are strongly sub-resolution. Their width can be re-adjusted "as long as a compensating adjustment is made in their transmission", so the width×transmission product is the effective invariant. The w·T product depends on edge orientation relative to polarization — [US 8078995, "Efficient isotropic modeling approach to incorporate electromagnetic effects into lithographic process simulations"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8078995); [US 8108802](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8108802)
- Ma & Arce (JOSA A, 2009) used the BLM inside inverse (binary-mask) optimization, treating the near field "as the superposition of the interior transmission areas and the boundary layers". This is an early example of a physics-based M3D model in ILT-type optimization — [Ma & Arce, U. Delaware PDF](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~arce/files/Publications/4-BinaryMask.pdf); [BIT Pure record](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/binary-mask-optimization-for-forward-lithography-based-on-the-bou/)

**Kernel / filter form of the M3D correction (Brion/ASML)**
- Liu, Cao et al., "Fast and accurate 3D mask model for full-chip OPC and verification," Proc. SPIE 6520, 65200R (2007), DOI 10.1117/12.712171. **EUV extensions of DDM**
- Mentor/Siemens: "the infrastructure for model-based compensation of mask shadowing effects exists within the domain decomposition method (DDM) model in the Calibre nmOPC tool." The same source notes that the simple "cosine rule" shadowing approximation is fast but inaccurate for across-slit CD errors — [Solid State Technology / Semiconductor Digest, "Tackling EUV lithography shadow distortions with OPC"](https://sst.semiconductor-digest.com/?p=33845)
- Angle interpolation of DDM signals: the patent computes edge-based DDM signals at a set of azimuthal illumination angles, simulates rigorously only a subset, and derives the rest from that subset. EUV's rotating plane of incidence across the ring slit is the motivation — [US 8539393, "Simulation and correction of mask shadowing effect"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8539393); see also [US 8601404, "Modeling EUV lithography shadowing effect"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8601404)
- A hybrid Hopkins-Abbe method extends DDM to oblique illumination for EUV crosstalk, but it still requires many near-field calculations — [US 10067425, "Correcting EUV crosstalk effects for lithography simulation"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10067425); the cost is characterised in [He et al., Light: Adv. Manuf. 2026](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.049)
- Berkeley (Neureuther group) dissertations treat mask-edge effects and chip-level modeling, with DDM and boundary-layer modeling as related approaches — [UC Berkeley dissertation, eScholarship qt0k12z798](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0k12z798/qt0k12z798.pdf)

**Convolution-kernel near-field compact model for high-NA EUV (Fraunhofer IISB)**
- Erdmann, Evanschitzky et al., "3D mask effects in high NA EUV imaging," Proc. SPIE 10957 (2019), DOI 10.1117/12.2515678. Proposes a **convolution-based compact model of the 3D-mask diffraction near field**, with kernels fitted to rigorous training data. The training patches are local features: convex corners, concave corners and edge segments. Verified with simulation experiments. This is the modern "edge/corner library" idea expressed as fitted kernels — [SPIE record](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10957/2515678/3D-mask-effects-in-high-NA-EUV-imaging/10.1117/12.2515678.short); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331750636_3D_mask_effects_in_high_NA_EUV_imaging)

### Inferences
- Three implementations share one structure: near field = thin-mask transmission + Σ (edge/corner-local correction). They differ in how the correction is parametrised. DDM uses rigorously tabulated edge/corner fields, the BLM uses strips with complex w·T, and kernel models (Brion 2007, IISB 2019) use fitted convolution kernels. All three are linear in the layout, so they slot into Hopkins/SOCS imaging and cost roughly one extra convolution set per kernel.
- The documented weaknesses are (a) small-feature/edge-to-edge coupling, handled by coupling patents and corner patches, and (b) illumination-angle dependence, handled by azimuth interpolation and hybrid Hopkins-Abbe. Both get worse for EUV, which has thick absorbers relative to λ and a 6°–9° chief-ray angle (CRA).

### Gaps
- No primary DDM paper from 2010–2026 with published speed or accuracy figures (for example, CD RMS against rigorous) was retrievable. Vendor figures for Calibre DDM, Tachyon M3D and Synopsys Proteus M3D appear only in patents or marketing. Treat the "edge library" terminology as industry usage, not verified from a specific paper.
- The exact Adam & Neureuther 2001 SPIE citation (volume/DOI) and Kostas Adam's later Mentor DDM papers (around 2003–2012) could not be fetched. They are cited only via patent references.

## Q2. M3D compact models for EUV: equivalent thin mask, effective mask, pupil filter / M3D kernels, spectrum correction and angle dependence

### Takeaway
For EUV, physics-based reduction takes three forms. (1) **Modified thin-mask models (TMM)** with an **equivalent reflecting layer** for the multilayer, plus analytic shadow/bias and best-mask-focus corrections. (2) **Diffraction-spectrum / pupil-domain corrections** that capture amplitude and phase changes of the orders, which behave like aberrations (telecentricity, best-focus shift, H–V bias). (3) **Structure-decomposed rigorous-lite models** that build the 3D spectrum from 2D or edge sub-problems and from angle-dependent frequency-domain functions. Fraunhofer IISB, imec and ASML characterised the physics (thick absorber ⇒ phase deformation; multilayer penetration ⇒ asymmetric near field). Chinese groups (SIOM, HUST) published the most explicit fast-model speed and accuracy numbers.

### Cited Findings
**Physics characterization (Fraunhofer IISB / imec / ASML)**

Technol. 6(3–4), 187–201 (2017), DOI 10.1515/aot-2017-0019 (review). Thick absorbers deform the wavefront/phase of incident light and generate **aberration-like effects**. 3D mask effects "can be partially compensated in the pupil plane" to reduce best-focus shift, H–V CD bias and image placement error. Current EUV uses a 6° CRA, and NA > 0.5 at 4× demagnification needs about 9° — [De Gruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/aot-2017-0019/html?lang=en); [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/advanced-optical-technologies/articles/10.1515/aot-2017-0019/full); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317104258_Characterization_and_mitigation_of_3D_mask_effects_in_extreme_ultraviolet_lithography)
- "Mask diffraction analysis and optimization for EUV masks," Proc. SPIE 7271, 72711E (2009), DOI 10.1117/12.814119 (IISB). Early diffraction-order analysis and pupil-plane compensation idea — [SPIE record](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/7271/72711E/Mask-diffraction-analysis-and-optimization-for-EUV-masks/10.1117/12.814119.full); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43808283_Mask_diffraction_analysis_and_optimization_for_extreme_ultraviolet_masks)

### Inferences
- "Spectrum correction" for EUV in the IISB/imec literature is mostly framed as **diffraction-order amplitude/phase analysis plus pupil-plane (aberration-like) compensation**. I found no named "spectrum-correction compact model" paper under that term. The closest compact formulations are the IISB 2019 convolution-kernel near-field model (Q1) and the SIOM angle-function model (AO 2020).
- Equivalent-layer TMMs are cheap and analytic but valid only within limited angle and pitch ranges (Cao 2011: ≤ 12°; Zhang 2018: corrections needed above 10°). That is marginal for high-NA, where the CRA is about 9° and angles span a larger range.

- I found no public accuracy figures (CD RMS against rigorous) for commercial EUV M3D compact models (ASML Tachyon/Brion, Siemens Calibre, Synopsys).

## Q3. Reduced basis methods (RBM) for Maxwell solvers in lithography and scatterometry (ZIB / JCMwave)

### Takeaway
ZIB/JCMwave (Pomplun, Burger, Zschiedrich, Schmidt) brought certified RBM to parametrised 3D FEM mask/scatterometry problems around 2009–2011. The method uses an expensive **offline** stage (snapshot FEM solves, greedy basis selection) and a cheap **online** stage (a small dense system per parameter). A key enabler was an **accelerated a-posteriori error estimator** based on subdomain residuals, needed because geometric parameters give affine expansions with many terms. The reported result: SMO with about 1,000 forward solves in about 1 hour instead of weeks.

### Cited Findings
- Pomplun, Burger, Zschiedrich, Schmidt, "Reduced basis method for computational lithography," Proc. SPIE (2009), arXiv:1011.2654. Targets the long run times of near-field simulation in lithography and optical metrology. RBM "allows to split up the solution process of a parameterized model into an expensive offline and a cheap online part" — [arXiv 1011.2654](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.2654)
- Pomplun, Burger, Zschiedrich, Schmidt (with Nikon co-authors), "Reduced basis method for source mask optimization," Proc. SPIE 7823, 78230E (Photomask/BACUS 2010), DOI 10.1117/12.866101, arXiv:1011.2902. RB results agree with exact FEM solutions in a convergence analysis, and **an optimization with about 1,000 forward solutions took on the order of an hour instead of several weeks** with the full model — [SPIE](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/7823/78230E/Reduced-basis-method-for-source-mask-optimization/10.1117/12.866101.short); [arXiv 1011.2902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2902); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47758261_Reduced_basis_method_for_source_mask_optimization)
- Pomplun & Schmidt, "Accelerated a posteriori error estimation for the reduced basis method with application to 3D electromagnetic scattering problems," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. (2010), DOI 10.1137/090760271. For affine expansions with **many terms**, where standard bounds are impractical, a **subdomain-residuum** estimator cuts online and offline time and memory "by large factors". Applied to 3D EM scattering on unbounded domains with a computational-lithography example — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/090760271); authorship per [search summary of ZIB work](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Reduced-Basis-Method-for-Electromagnetic-Field-Pomplun-Schmidt/05e8290f35f35d00dbd138d402dc7ef95cca4300)
- Pomplun & Schmidt, "Reduced basis method for electromagnetic field computations" — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Reduced-Basis-Method-for-Electromagnetic-Field-Pomplun-Schmidt/05e8290f35f35d00dbd138d402dc7ef95cca4300); ZIB project page "Reduced basis computation of highly complex geometries" — [ZIB](https://www.zib.de/node/3914)
- Extensions: Hammerschmidt et al. applied RBM to FinFET scattering problems (metrology), and Schmidt et al. to nanophotonic mask optimization — per [search summary of ZIB publication listing](https://www.zib.de/node/3914)
- Related ZIB/JCMwave EUV work: "Rigorous simulations of 3D patterns on extreme ultraviolet lithography masks" (Burger, Zschiedrich, Pomplun, Schmidt et al., 2011), arXiv:1105.6052 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.6052); "Advanced finite-element methods for design and analysis of nanooptical structures: Applications," arXiv:1304.4356 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.4356); "Method for fast computation of angular light scattering spectra from 2D periodic arrays," arXiv:1603.04169 (relevant to angle sweeps) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.04169)
- JCMsuite–Dr.LiTHO coupling: Burger, Zschiedrich, Schmidt, Evanschitzky, Erdmann (2008, arXiv:0906.1896) interfaced the JCMsuite FEM solver with Dr.LiTHO imaging/resist and benchmarked Dr.LiTHO's waveguide solver against FEM — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0906.1896)
- Parallel certified-RBM theory for Maxwell: Chen, Hesthaven, Maday, Rodríguez, "Certified reduced basis methods and output bounds for the harmonic Maxwell's equations," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 32(2), 970–996 (2010) — [UMass Dartmouth repository](https://repository.lib.umassd.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/CERTIFIED-REDUCED-BASIS-METHODS-AND-OUTPUT/9914437507701301); improved evaluation of the RB error estimator (round-off-robust): Casenave, Ern, Lelièvre, M2AN 48(1), 207 (2014), arXiv:1212.0970 — [Numdam](https://www.numdam.org/item/M2AN_2014__48_1_207_0); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.0970)

### Inferences
- RBM fits *parametric* problems: a fixed topology with a few geometric or material parameters (CD, sidewall angle, thickness, incidence angle). That is why it was used in SMO on a fixed clip and in scatterometry. It does not transfer to free-form, layout-wide ILT, where the parameter space is the whole layout. In OPC flows this pushes RBM-type reduction into **library generation** (for example, edge/corner signals over a range of angles and stack parameters), not into the online full-chip model.
- Geometric parametrisation leads to non-affine operators. This is the reason the many-term affine expansion and subdomain-residual estimator (SIAM 2010) were essential.

### Gaps
- I found no published RBM-for-lithography paper after about 2012 from ZIB/JCMwave. Later JCMwave work moved toward Bayesian optimization and metrology. No public evidence was found that RBM is used in a production OPC M3D model.
- Specific offline cost figures (snapshot count, basis dimension N, online time per solve) for the 2009/2010 papers were not retrievable from abstracts.

## Q4. Fast RCWA and rigorous-solver acceleration: Fourier factorization, S-matrix reuse, low-rank/Krylov, parameter sweeps; fast FDTD/FEM/waveguide alternatives

### Takeaway
Two things make RCWA practical for masks. **Correct Fourier factorization** (Li 1996/97, with the normal-vector refinement of Schuster 2007 and Götz 2008) gives fast convergence in harmonic count, especially for metallic/absorbing 2D-periodic structures. **S-matrix storage and reuse** lets layer eigen-solutions be reused across thickness and stack variants. For EUV the Fraunhofer IISB solver line evolved from the **waveguide method** to an **extended RCWA** coupled with a vectorial Abbe imager. In parallel, iterative volume solvers (**modified Born series**, and imec's **pseudo-spectral frequency-domain (PSFD) method with background-field decomposition and a Green's-function preconditioner**) target large-area 3D EUV clips at about 100× FDTD speed.

### Cited Findings
**Fourier factorization**
- Li (JOSA A 1996, 1997) showed that poor RCWA convergence for crossed gratings came from "erroneous application of factorization rules to discrete Fourier transformations". Li's 1997 SPIE paper reports much faster convergence than the old modal method by Fourier expansion (MMFE) and the first convergent results for metallic crossed gratings — [SPIE 3010, "Modal method by Fourier expansion for modeling crossed gratings"](https://astronomicaltelescopes.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/3010/0000/Modal-method-by-Fourier-expansion-for-modeling-crossed-gratings/10.1117/12.274421.full); [arXiv 1507.06364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06364)
- Schuster et al., JOSA A 24, 2880 (2007): a structure-dependent normal-vector (NV) field. Götz (Goetz) et al., Opt. Express (2008), "Normal vector method for the RCWA with automated vector field generation", gives an algorithm for arbitrary shapes — [Uni Stuttgart PUMA record](https://puma.ub.uni-stuttgart.de/bibtex/2c449f6f61c6914ce7be77ffd8a036626/vogelfrau); a secondary summary claims the NV method improves significantly over Li's zigzag and Moharam–Gaylord formulations (unverified) — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/71738164/Simulating_Grating_Diffraction_Using_the_Normal_Vector_Method)

### Inferences
- For EUV the multilayer is a laterally invariant 1D stack. Every fast rigorous scheme found exploits this: equivalent layer (compact models), analytic background (PSFD), and RCWA layer S-matrices. These are reused across all patterns and angles, and only the absorber region is solved per pattern.
- MBS and PSFD are Krylov/iterative solvers whose warm-start capability (MBS "slightly altered pattern" speedup; PSFD+neural-operator warm start) suits OPC/ILT inner loops where the mask changes incrementally.

- PSFD speedup figures (against RCWA/FDTD) and the formulation of the Green's-function preconditioner were not retrievable (full text blocked).

## Q5. Handling illumination-angle dependence: Hopkins breakdown, multi-angle M3D kernels, interpolation across angles

### Takeaway
Hopkins imaging assumes the mask spectrum just shifts with the illumination angle (diffraction independent of incidence angle). M3D breaks this. The breakdown is modest in DUV (Brion's 2007 "non-Hopkins oblique incidence" correction) and severe in EUV, because of the 6° CRA (about 9° for high-NA anamorphic designs), multilayer penetration and thick absorbers. The practical options, ordered by cost, are: (i) Hopkins with a single M3D kernel set; (ii) a few M3D kernel sets for groups of source points or azimuths, with interpolation between rigorously simulated angles; (iii) hybrid Hopkins-Abbe; (iv) full Abbe with per-source-point rigorous spectra (the IISB/Dr.LiTHO reference practice). Option (iv) is accurate but needs one rigorous solve per source point.

### Cited Findings
- The Hopkins approximation assumes mask diffraction does not depend on illumination angle, and thick-mask effects make it "no longer viable" for EUV. The hybrid Hopkins-Abbe extension of DDM still requires many near-field calculations. Abbe imaging requires a separate simulation per source point — [He et al., "Full-chip EUV curvilinear mask optimization," Light: Adv. Manuf. 2026, DOI 10.37188/lam.2026.049](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.049); [PDF preview](https://www.light-am.com/article/pdf/preview/LAM2026020047.pdf)
- High-NA work reports that the Hopkins assumption breaks down at least in some cases — [Erdmann, Evanschitzky et al., Proc. SPIE 10957 (2019)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331750636_3D_mask_effects_in_high_NA_EUV_imaging)
- Liu, Cao et al. (2007) explicitly model **non-Hopkins oblique-incidence effects** in a full-chip 3D mask model and beat a thick-mask model with Hopkins treatment — [SPIE 6520 65200R](https://proceedings.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6520/65200R/Fast-and-accurate-3D-mask-model-for-full-chip-OPC/10.1117/12.712171.full)

### Inferences
- Angle dependence is smooth over the small angular range of an EUV illuminator (a few degrees about the CRA). This is why low-order interpolation across a few rigorous angles (patent US 8539393) and angle-parametrised analytic functions (AO 2020) work. Accuracy degrades for high-NA, where the angular span roughly doubles.
- In compact-model terms, the "multi-angle M3D kernel" is a source-partitioned Hopkins model: the source is split into K regions, each with its own M3D-corrected TCC/SOCS kernel set, so cost scales roughly linearly with K. No public paper giving K-versus-accuracy trade-offs was found.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper that quantifies source-point grouping or angle-interpolation error (for example, CD error vs. number of rigorous angles) for EUV M3D. The evidence is patents and one Chinese fast-model paper.
- Mesilhy/Erdmann angle-dependence results for high-NA (around 2021–2024) could be cited only via a workshop poster.

## Q6. Synthesis: method-by-method summary and most advanced current practice (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Current practice is layered. **Full-chip OPC/ILT** uses DDM/edge-corner-library or fitted M3D-kernel corrections to the thin mask, with source-partitioned or angle-interpolated signals for EUV and coupling corrections for tight pitches. These run in vendor tools: Calibre DDM is confirmed; Brion/ASML kernels are confirmed via patents. **Model building, verification and high-NA SMO** use per-source-point rigorous spectra (extended RCWA/waveguide + vectorial Abbe in Dr.LiTHO; FEM in JCMsuite). The 2023–2026 research frontier is **fast iterative 3D solvers with background decomposition and warm starts** (HUST MBS, imec PSFD), increasingly paired with ML surrogates or warm starts (out of scope).

### Inferences
- The physics-based reduction hierarchy runs: rigorous per-angle (FEM/RCWA/waveguide) → parametric ROM (RBM) or library tabulation (DDM edge/corner signals at a few angles) → linear kernel correction of the thin mask (BLM/M3D kernels) → Hopkins/SOCS. Each step trades generality for speed. EUV forces angle information back into the lower levels.
- The documented speedups span about 65× (variable separation), about 100× (MBS vs. FDTD), about 390× (angle-function model vs. RCWA) and "weeks → 1 h" (RBM in SMO). None of these alone reaches full-chip OPC throughput, which is why linear edge/kernel corrections still dominate production.

### Gaps
- I found no public, side-by-side benchmark of M3D compact models (DDM vs. kernel vs. TMM) against a common rigorous reference for high-NA EUV.
- Vendor implementation details for production M3D, including how many angle partitions are used and the calibration procedure, are not public beyond patents.
- The searches surfaced no thesis focused specifically on M3D reduced-order modeling from 2015 onward (for example, an FAU Erlangen/IISB thesis by Mesilhy or another student). Such a thesis may exist but was not found.
