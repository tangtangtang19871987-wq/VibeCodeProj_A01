## 1. TCC decomposition: SOCS / optimal coherent kernels, truncation error, and alternatives to eigendecomposition

### Takeaway
SOCS (eigendecomposition of the Hermitian PSD TCC, keeping the leading kernels) is still the workhorse. By Eckart–Young, the truncation error in Hilbert–Schmidt norm is exactly governed by the discarded eigenvalues. Work since 2010 has focused on (a) building the TCC and its leading eigenpairs faster (stacked pupil-shift matrix, fixed-point or partial eigen-solvers, predefined analytical bases), (b) beating the eigenbasis at fixed kernel count with **non-coherent kernels** (Rosenbluth, SPIE 2017, at least 5× lower error), and (c) understanding truncation **artifacts** (Yamazoe & Ho, SPIE 2026: negative intensity and broken periodicity, cured by sampling finer than the Nyquist grid).

### Cited Findings
**Classic SOCS and truncation error**
- The TCC is Hermitian and positive semidefinite, so it can be eigendecomposed into weighted coherent modes. SOCS keeps only the leading terms. — [arXiv 2602.15036, "Transforming Computational Lithography with AC and AI" (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15036); [K-Litho toolset (TCC/SOCS kernel derivation, defocus as input), GitHub](https://github.com/LithoSimulator/K-Litho)
- **Yamazoe & Ho, "Image artifacts by TCC truncation," Proc. SPIE 13979 (Optical and EUV Nanolithography XXXIX), 139790F, Feb 24 2026, DOI 10.1117/12.3091052 (TSMC).** In SOCS only the leading TCC eigenpairs are used. The eigenvalue spectrum normally decays fast, but truncation can cause negative image intensity and non-periodic images from periodic masks. These artifacts are avoided by using a grid finer than the optical image's Nyquist grid. The paper gives a mathematical error analysis; the snippet shows the truncation error scaling as O(λ_{k+1}) for k kernels, but the exponent was garbled in extraction, so check the PDF. — [preprint PDF](http://klho.github.io/pubs/docs/yamazoe-2026-proc-spie.pdf); [SPIE vol. 13979 listing](https://spie.org/Publications/Proceedings/Volume/13979); [AL26 conference page](https://spie.org/AL26/conferencedetails/optical-and-euv-nanolithography)
- General result: truncating to the leading N modes minimizes the Hilbert–Schmidt error over all rank-N approximations (Eckart–Young), and the squared error equals the sum of the squared discarded eigenvalues. This comes from a physics arXiv paper, not a litho paper, but it is the standard bound applied to the TCC. — [arXiv 2608.09932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09932). Entrywise (pointwise) low-rank error bounds for kernel matrices: [NeurIPS 2024, "Entrywise error bounds for low-rank approximations of kernel matrices"](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/e55653539c9a9aa096a1fc8ca77ff413-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Industry kernel counts: a "Delta TCC for fast sensitivity model computation" patent (US 8379991) says the conventional setting of **64 TCC kernels** is inadequate for capturing perturbations, and that **up to 1024 kernels** are used in some cases. — [US 8379991](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8379991)
- Raising the kernel count N improves SOCS accuracy but also raises run time and memory, which motivates automatic choice of N. — [US 7127699, "Method for optimizing a number of kernels used in a sum of coherent sources for OPC"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7127699)
- TCC truncation combined with floating-point error in iterative eigen-solvers makes kernels gradually lose their symmetry. A projection step restores the symmetry. — [US 12306543, "Optical imaging method, device and system for photolithography system"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12306543)

**Faster TCC construction and eigen-solvers**
- **Yamazoe, "Computation theory of partially coherent imaging by stacked pupil shift matrix," JOSA A 25(12):3111 (2008).** Pupil functions shifted per source point are stacked into a matrix P. SVD of P gives the TCC eigenpairs directly, without forming the 4-D TCC (TCC = P†P). — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-25-12-3111)
- "Fast and accurate computation of partially coherent imaging by stacked pupil shift operator," Proc. SPIE 7488 (2009): matrix-multiplication TCC assembly is **about 10× faster on average**. The decomposition is applied to TCC = P†P so that mask and resist effects can be folded into the kernels. — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009SPIE.7488E..3GL/abstract); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253665322_Fast_and_accurate_computation_of_partially_coherent_imaging_by_stacked_pupil_shift_operator)
- Related Yamazoe-line matrix formulations from 2010: "Two matrix approaches for aerial image formation obtained by extending and modifying the transmission cross coefficients," JOSA A 27(6):1311 (2010) — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-27-6-1311); "Expansion of image interaction by the eigenfunction of the transmission cross coefficient…," JOSA A 27(9):1920 (2010) — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-27-9-1920); "Fast fine-pixel aerial image calculation in partially coherent imaging by matrix representation of modified Hopkins equation," Applied Optics 49(20):3909 (2010) — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-49-20-3909). (Authorship of the latter two was not shown in snippets; verify.)
- A fixed-point algorithm computes only the needed TCC eigenvectors, which speeds up runtime (ResearchGate listing, no full text). — [ResearchGate summary via search](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253665322_Fast_and_accurate_computation_of_partially_coherent_imaging_by_stacked_pupil_shift_operator)
- "Fast TCC algorithm for the model building of high NA lithography simulation." — [ResearchGate 253788863](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253788863_Fast_TCC_algorithm_for_the_model_building_of_high_NA_lithography_simulation); earlier background: "Fast approximation of transfer cross coefficient for OPC" — [ResearchGate 23261497](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23261497_Fast_approximation_of_transfer_cross_coefficient_for_optical_proximity_correction)

**Alternatives to the eigenbasis**
- **Rosenbluth (per search attribution), "Decomposition of the TCC using non-coherent kernels for faster calculation of lithographic images," Proc. SPIE 10147 (2017), DOI 10.1117/12.2261223.** Replaces the coherent (eigen) kernels with non-coherent kernels. In 1D tests, image error **routinely drops by at least 5×** at a given kernel count in the 10–100 range. — [SPIE](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10147/1/Decomposition-of-the-TCC-using-non-coherent-kernels-for-faster/10.1117/12.2261223.short)
- **Predefined analytical kernels (EIPBN 2012, "Fast aerial image simulation for partially coherent systems by TCC decomposition with analytical kernels").** The 4-D TCC SVD is slow and must be redone whenever the TCC size changes. Projecting the TCC onto fixed orthogonal bases (circle sampling functions, Zernike polynomials, Fourier series) keeps the projection matrix fixed for a given system. — [EIPBN 2012 abstract](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2012/papers/P18-01.pdf); [ResearchGate 258672561](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258672561_Fast_aerial_image_simulations_for_partially_coherent_systems_by_transmission_cross_coefficient_decomposition_with_analytical_kernels)
- **Abbe-PCA ("Abbe–Hopkins") compact kernels.** Per-source-point PSFs form a correlation matrix, and PCA eigendecomposition yields a much smaller set of "Abbe kernels" that still models the system accurately. — [ResearchGate 241531182, "Abbe-PCA (Abbe-Hopkins): microlithography aerial image analytical compact kernel generation based on PCA"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241531182_Abbe-PCA_Abbe-Hopkins_microlithography_aerial_image_analytical_compact_kernel_generation_based_on_principle_component_analysis); [US 8136054, "Compact Abbe's kernel generation using PCA"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8136054). A ScienceDirect robust-SMO paper credits Chang & Chen with applying PCA to the Abbe model for SMO efficiency. — [Optics & Laser Tech. 2012, "Robust pixel-based source and mask optimization for inverse lithography"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0030399212003015)
- 2D-TCC approximation as an RET or mask-optimization tool (Yamazoe et al., SPIE 6924, 2008; background). — [SPIE 6924 69240F](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6924/69240F/Mask-optimization-for-arbitrary-patterns-with-2D-TCC-resolution-enhancement/10.1117/12.771943.short); [ResearchGate 241394390](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241394390_Resolution_enhancement_by_aerial_image_approximation_with_2D-TCC)
- High-NA EUV: **Tanabe et al., JM3 25(3):031604 (2026)** extend the TCC formula to polarization and mask-3D effects, so the Hopkins/SOCS route stays usable for high-NA EUV. — [Science Tokyo repository](https://t2r2.star.titech.ac.jp/rrws/file/CTT100942554/ATD100000413/)

### Inferences
- Several regimes are each well served. SOCS with Eckart–Young-controlled truncation is optimal **in TCC norm**. Rosenbluth's non-coherent kernels show that TCC-norm optimality is *not* image-error optimality at a fixed kernel count, which makes this the most interesting post-2010 "beyond eigen" result. Yamazoe & Ho show that sampling or grid choice interacts with truncation and must be part of any error budget.
- Randomized SVD, Tucker or tensor-train TCC compression, and sparse TCC appear as general numerical tools, but I found no 2010–2026 litho-specific paper on them in this session (see Gaps).

- No full text of Yamazoe & Ho (2026) was reachable, so the exact truncation-error formula and numbers are unverified.
- I found no litho-specific paper on **randomized SVD**, **Tucker/tensor-train** TCC decomposition, or **sparse/compressed TCC** in 2010–2026 searches. This may be a real literature gap or a search limitation.

## 2. PARAMETRIC imaging across focus, dose, NA, source, polarization and aberrations (priority question)

### Takeaway
Physics-exact parametric reduction has three main branches. (i) **Analytic defocus or process-window expansion.** The intensity is (nearly) even in defocus, so I(z) ≈ I₀ + z²I₂ (+ dose scaling). Kernels for each coefficient are precomputed (Peng Yu, Pan & Mack 2006, about 2–3× nominal cost; Brion-style process-window patents also add NA). (ii) **Aberration perturbation kernels.** The TCC is written as an ideal kernel set plus Zernike-weighted perturbation kernel sets (linear, or quadratic as in the HUST "quadratic aberration model" built on cross-triple correlation), giving sensitivity kernels. (iii) **Source parameterization for SMO.** Abbe-form source gradients, hybrid Hopkins–Abbe with precomputed per-source TCCs, PCA or Zernike source bases, and subspace or compressive restriction of source pixels. I found no explicit 2010–2026 paper on *shared-basis SOCS kernels across focus* or *TCC interpolation across focus*.

### Inferences
- The exact way to make focus cheap is to expand the *pupil* phase (defocus or Zernike) and push each Taylor or Zernike coefficient's contribution into its own TCC, i.e. its own kernel set. Even-symmetry in defocus removes the linear term. Error is controlled by Taylor order and by per-coefficient kernel truncation, but Delta-TCC experience shows that perturbation TCCs need many more kernels (more than 64, up to 1024) than nominal ones.
- For source changes, Abbe is exactly linear in source-pixel weights (I = Σ_s w_s I_s). Gradients with respect to the source are therefore just per-point images, and the real ROM problem is compressing the per-point image set (PCA/Abbe-PCA, Zernike or parametric source bases, diffraction-subspace pixel pruning, or hybrid per-source TCCs).
- An interpolation-in-focus or common-basis SOCS approach (one shared eigenbasis with focus-dependent small coefficient matrices) follows directly from writing TCC(z) = Φ C(z) Φ†. I found no 2010–2026 paper explicitly benchmarking it, so it should be presented as an open or industrial-practice item.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that explicitly does **TCC or kernel interpolation across focus** or **shared-basis SOCS kernels across parameter values**. Search for "focus-dependent kernels" returned only patents and tools.
- Speedup and accuracy numbers for the Zernike perturbation-kernel patents and the HUST quadratic model were not available from snippets.
- Unverified leads: Yamazoe's matrix-pupil formalism applied through focus; Evanschitzky & Erdmann (Fraunhofer IISB, Dr.LiTHO) fast EUV imaging and M3D compact models; Wu, Liu & Lam aberration-aware OPC/SMO papers; Ma & Arce *Computational Lithography* (Wiley 2010) Abbe/Hopkins gradient derivations; Poonawala & Milanfar (IEEE TIP 2007) ILT gradients. None was confirmed in this session.

## 3. Incremental / local imaging: local mask updates, optical ambit, band-limiting and Nyquist, multi-resolution, FFT tiling and stitching

### Takeaway
Linearity of each SOCS channel (the field is a mask convolved with a kernel, then |·|²) makes local updates exact in principle: Δfield_k = kernel_k * Δmask within the kernel support (ambit). Literature evidence is mainly (a) lookup-table or edge-based schemes whose cost scales with the number of local polygon edges and which are "well suited for OPC perturbations", (b) halo-based tiling for full-chip and GPU, and (c) the Yamazoe & Ho result that SOCS on a grid finer than the image Nyquist grid avoids truncation artifacts. Optical ambit is about 1 µm at the 45 nm node.

### Cited Findings
- **Lookup-table partially coherent imaging (Cobb & Zakhor line), "Fast sparse aerial-image calculation for OPC," Proc. SPIE 2621 (1995), background.** Cost per image sample is proportional to the number of polygon edges in the local mask region. The method is particularly suited to mask perturbations such as those OPC produces. — [SPIE 2621](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/2621/0000/Fast-sparse-aerial-image-calculation-for-OPC/10.1117/12.228208.full)
- Optical influence radius of about 1 µm for OPC kernels at the 45 nm node. OPC/ILT flows clip a finite context window around targets. — [MaskOpt, arXiv 2512.20655 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20655v1)
- Full-chip tiling: light intensity at a wafer location depends on a large surrounding mask area, so tiles need halos. Conventional OPC/ILT halos can be large, which lowers simulation area efficiency. Stitching at the end needs special fixes for boundary inconsistencies, while freezing predecessors' solutions limits degrees of freedom. — [US 11340584, "Synchronized parallel tile computation for large area lithography simulation"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11340584); [US 8490034 / 8938696, "Techniques of OPC using GPU"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8490034)
- Grid and Nyquist: SOCS truncation artifacts (negative intensity, broken periodicity) are avoided by computing on a grid finer than the Nyquist grid of the optical image. — [Yamazoe & Ho, SPIE 13979 (2026)](http://klho.github.io/pubs/docs/yamazoe-2026-proc-spie.pdf)
- "Fast fine-pixel aerial image calculation" via a matrix form of a modified Hopkins equation (Applied Optics 49(20):3909, 2010) targets fine-pixel output without paying the full fine-grid FFT cost. — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-49-20-3909)
- Ultra-fast aerial image simulation using **wavelength scaling and FFT** (T.-S. Gau et al., JM3, 2023). Seen only as a citation in a GitHub README; not verified. — [quarterwave0/LithographySimulator](https://github.com/quarterwave0/LithographySimulator)
- A stitchless full-chip splitting method on modest GPU resources (DAMO, ICCAD 2020; ML-based OPC, cited only for its tiling scheme). — [arXiv 2008.00806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.00806)
- Local correction by matching segments against precomputed "typical case" segments (approximate, not exact). — [US 6854104, "First approximation for OPC significant speed-up"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6854104)

### Inferences
- The aerial image is band-limited to spatial frequency 2NA/λ (intensity) and each coherent field to NA/λ (with source σ included in the TCC support). So the exact-reconstruction pixel is λ/(4NA(1+σ))-type for intensity. The Yamazoe & Ho result implies that SOCS-truncated images are *not* guaranteed band-limited-consistent at that pixel, so a safety oversampling factor is part of the error budget.
- Exact incremental update per kernel: E_k ← E_k + h_k * Δm (cost proportional to the support of Δm ⊕ kernel ambit), then I = Σ λ_k |E_k|² recomputed locally. This is what edge/polygon-lookup methods exploit.

### Gaps
- I found no 2010–2026 peer-reviewed paper benchmarking **incremental SOCS updates** or **hierarchical / multi-resolution aerial-image computation** (e.g., wavelet or multigrid). Patents dominate.
- Exact halo widths used by commercial tools were not found.

## 4. Fast imaging for full-chip OPC and verification: polygon / edge kernel lookup, convolution tables, error characteristics; vendor practice

### Takeaway
Full-chip OPC engines combine SOCS kernels with precomputed convolution tables over polygon primitives (edges and corners). The cost per evaluation point scales with nearby edge count, and the error comes from kernel truncation, table sampling and ambit cutoff. Public vendor detail is sparse: patents from (likely) ASML/Brion describe process-window TCC expansions, Delta-TCC sensitivity kernels, Zernike perturbation kernel sets and GPU tiling. Brion's Tachyon is publicly described only at the level of "first-principles, full-chip FEM". I found no public technical description of Calibre's imaging kernels.

### Inferences
- Error characteristics of edge/corner lookup = SOCS truncation error (bounded by the discarded eigenvalue tail) + table quantization/interpolation error + ambit cutoff error (energy of kernels beyond the table radius) + polygon decomposition error for curvilinear shapes. The last term grows with curvilinear (ILT) masks, which is one reason curvilinear full-chip flows are moving to pixel/FFT + GPU (Light: Adv. Manuf. 2026 full-chip EUV curvilinear MO).

- No quantitative accuracy figures (e.g., CD error in nm versus kernel count) for edge-lookup methods were found in 2010–2026 sources.

## 5. Most advanced / most recent methods: summary table (with limits)

### Takeaway
The leading physics-exact or controlled-error advances in 2010–2026 are: non-coherent TCC kernels (2017); analytical predefined kernel bases (2012); stacked-pupil and matrix TCC construction (2008–2010); hybrid Hopkins–Abbe gradients for SMO and polarization (2020); Zernike, parametric and subspace source representations (2012–2018); HUST quadratic aberration and CTC models (2012–2020); vector and polarization-extended TCC for high-NA EUV (2026); and a rigorous analysis of TCC-truncation artifacts with an oversampling rule (2026).

### Inferences
- The most consequential *recent* exact-method advances are Rosenbluth's non-coherent kernels (accuracy per kernel) and Yamazoe & Ho's truncation-artifact analysis (error control). For parametric imaging, the state of the art in open literature is still Taylor/Zernike perturbation kernels plus Abbe-linear source parameterization. Commercial process-window kernels are known mainly from patents.
