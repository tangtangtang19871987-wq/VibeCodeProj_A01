## Q1. Coherent-mode decomposition (CMD) of partially coherent light: Wolf modes, X-ray/synchrotron CMD, mixed-state ptychography, and how many modes are needed

### Takeaway
The cross-spectral density (mutual intensity) is a Hermitian, positive semidefinite kernel. Its Mercer/Karhunen–Loève expansion (Wolf's coherent modes) is the optimal low-rank representation in the L2 sense. Synchrotron optics now uses this routinely (COMSYL, WOFRY 1D-CMD, XRT/SRW-based CMD) to replace many incoherent propagations with a few coherent ones. At 4th-generation storage rings the hard X-ray coherent fraction is only a few percent, so the number of modes needed grows with photon energy: about 10 at 12 keV, more above that. Mixed-state ptychography (Thibault & Menzel 2013) applies the same low-rank density-matrix idea inside a reconstruction. This is the same mathematics as Hopkins-TCC SOCS in lithography, so the transferable ideas are about computing the modes (separability, matrix-free eigensolvers, propagating the modes instead of the TCC) rather than the decomposition itself.

### Cited Findings
**Foundations**
- Wolf showed that in the space–frequency domain the cross-spectral density W(r1,r2,ω) of any statistically stationary field has a Mercer expansion W = Σ λ_n φ_n*(r1) φ_n(r2) with λ_n ≥ 0. Each mode φ_n is a fully coherent field that propagates independently, so partially coherent propagation becomes a sum of coherent propagations weighted by λ_n. E. Wolf, "New theory of partial coherence in the space–frequency domain. Part I: spectra and cross spectra of steady-state sources," JOSA 72(3), 343–351 (1982), doi:10.1364/JOSA.72.000343 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.72.000343)
- Vartanyants & Singer used Gaussian Schell-model CMD to quantify coherence of 3rd-generation synchrotrons and XFELs (coherent fraction = λ_0/Σλ_n; number of modes ~ ratio of source size × divergence to λ/4π). I.A. Vartanyants & A. Singer, "Coherence properties of hard x-ray synchrotron sources and x-ray free-electron lasers," New J. Phys. 12, 035004 (2010), doi:10.1088/1367-2630/12/3/035004 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/12/3/035004)

**Numerical CMD for undulator radiation (beamline simulation)**
- Glass & Sanchez del Rio introduced a numerical method that decomposes undulator radiation into coherent modes. It is implemented in the open-source COMSYL code ("COherent Modes for SYnchrotron Light"). The method builds the full cross-spectral density from electron-beam and undulator parameters and diagonalizes it, and the first eigenvalue gives the coherent fraction directly. M. Glass & M. Sanchez del Rio, "Coherent modes of X-ray beams emitted by undulators in new storage rings," EPL 119(3), 34004 (2017), doi:10.1209/0295-5075/119/34004 [V title/venue; DOI pattern K] — [summary via search: Sanchez del Rio FLS2018 talk](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/fls2018/talks/thp2wd03_talk.pdf); [arXiv 1801.07542, "About the coherent fraction of synchrotron emission"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.07542)
- COMSYL was applied to the 4 m U18 undulator at the ESRF-EBS (low-β and high-β straight sections). The coherent fraction at new-generation rings, "although much improved … is still of the order of a few percent at hard X-rays", so beamline design still has to model partial coherence. — [arXiv 1801.07542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.07542); [IUCr JSR 2022 ay5600](https://journals.iucr.org/s/issues/2022/06/00/ay5600/)
- **Cost and the separability reduction:** the full 2D (4D cross-spectral density) COMSYL calculation is heavy. ESRF later proposed a 1D CMD that factorizes horizontal and vertical (W(x1,y1,x2,y2) ≈ W_x(x1,x2)·W_y(y1,y2)) and is light enough to run interactively on a laptop. Sanchez del Rio et al., J. Synchrotron Rad. 29(6) (2022), IUCr code ay5600 [V venue/issue; title K: "A fast and lightweight tool for partially coherent beamline simulations in fourth-generation storage rings based on coherent mode decomposition"] — [IUCr](https://journals.iucr.org/s/issues/2022/06/00/ay5600/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/9df913687aae4717bdd03ca9e9a3127d)
- The WOFRY package (Wave Optics FRamework in pYthon, in OASYS) does partially coherent beamline simulation for 4th-generation rings by propagating CMD modes one at a time through 1D wavefront propagators. "WOFRY: a package for partially coherent beamline simulations in fourth-generation storage rings," arXiv:2410.01338 (2024) [V existence; content not fetched because arxiv.org was unreachable] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01338)
- Khubbutdinov, Menushenkov & Vartanyants ran XRT simulations together with an analytical CMD for ~10 pm·rad rings over 500 eV–50 keV. Results: the diffraction limit (emittance λ/4π-type) is reached only around 500 eV (soft X-ray). **About ten modes contribute at 12 keV**, with more at higher energy. Relative energy spread up to 2×10⁻³ lowers the coherence further, and the diffraction limit at high energies would need ≤1 pm·rad. R. Khubbutdinov, A.P. Menushenkov, I.A. Vartanyants, "Coherence properties of the high-energy fourth-generation X-ray synchrotron sources," J. Synchrotron Rad. 26, 1851–1862 (2019), doi:10.1107/S1600577519013079 [V] — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6853378/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03671)
- Related compact representation for propagation: Lindberg & Kim derived compact (Gaussian–Hermite-type) representations of partially coherent undulator radiation for wave propagation. R.R. Lindberg & K.-J. Kim, "Compact representations of partially coherent undulator radiation suitable for wave propagation," Phys. Rev. ST Accel. Beams 18, 090702 (2015), doi:10.1103/PhysRevSTAB.18.090702 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTAB.18.090702)

**Mixed-state (low-rank density matrix) reconstruction**
- Thibault & Menzel gave a general approach to diffractive imaging systems "that can be described as low-rank mixed states". It is based on ptychography and closely related to quantum state tomography. Probe and/or object are modelled as an incoherent sum of a few orthogonal modes and recovered jointly, which handles partial coherence, quantum mixtures and fast stationary stochastic processes (vibrations, switching, flows). P. Thibault & A. Menzel, "Reconstructing state mixtures from diffraction measurements," Nature 494, 68–71 (2013), doi:10.1038/nature11806 [V] — [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nature11806); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v494y2013i7435d10.1038_nature11806.html)
- Low-rank mutual-intensity recovery with nuclear-norm/low-entropy priors: Tian, Lee, Oh & Barbastathis gave the first experimental compressive reconstruction of the mutual intensity from undersampled phase-space tomography. They relied on a "low-entropy source" assumption, i.e. few coherent modes, used low-rank matrix completion theory (Gross et al., PRL 2010, quantum state tomography), and validated against the van Cittert–Zernike ground truth. L. Tian, J. Lee, S.B. Oh, G. Barbastathis, "Experimental compressive phase space tomography," Opt. Express 20(8), 8296–8308 (2012), doi:10.1364/OE.20.008296 [V venue/pages; DOI K] — [arXiv 1109.1322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.1322)
- Partially coherent phase retrieval with joint source recovery (source = incoherent sum of point emitters/modes) in quantitative phase microscopy: Z. Zhong, L. Tian, M. Varma, L. Waller, "Nonlinear optimization algorithm for partially coherent phase retrieval and source recovery," IEEE Trans. Comput. Imaging 2(3), 310–322 (2016) [K] — [IEEE TCI](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7469385) (URL from memory, unverified)

### Inferences
- **How many modes:** in X-ray work the count follows the source's coherent fraction. A few modes cover soft X-ray at diffraction-limited rings, about 10 at 12 keV (Khubbutdinov 2019), and tens or more at higher energies or for 3rd-generation sources. For lithography the analogue is SOCS kernel count, set by the illumination σ/shape. The X-ray literature shows a coherent-fraction-like spectral-decay criterion (Σ_{n≤N} λ_n / Σ λ_n ≥ 1−ε) is the standard truncation rule, and it carries over directly to choosing the TCC rank.
- **Transferable trick 1 (separability):** the ESRF 1D-CMD factorizes the cross-spectral density into x and y parts, which cuts a 4D eigenproblem to two 2D ones. Lithographic TCCs are not separable for annular or freeform sources, but for rectangular, Manhattan-symmetric (dipole/quadrupole) sources a separable or low-tensor-rank approximation of the TCC (Tucker/CP format) could cut the cost of building it. This is an inference; no lithography source for it was checked here.
- **Transferable trick 2 (propagate modes, not kernels):** CMD codes compute modes at the source and propagate each mode through the optics. For through-focus or through-aberration lithography the equivalent is to decompose the source once (source-mode decomposition, as in the Abbe–SOCS hybrid) and push the pupil/aberration variation into the coherent propagator. This avoids a new TCC eigendecomposition for every focus/aberration point.
- Mixed-state ptychography shows the low-rank structure can be *estimated* jointly with the object. The ILT analogue is joint source-mask optimization in low-rank (mode) form.

### Gaps
- The exact speedups in the COMSYL (2017) and 1D-CMD (2022) papers (CPU time, memory, number of modes kept for EBS cases) could not be pulled because journals.iucr.org and arxiv.org did not resolve from this environment.
- No paper checked here gives a quantitative "modes vs. accuracy" rule for mixed-state ptychography. Practitioners commonly report 3–10 probe modes, but this session did not verify that.
- SRW-based CMD (Chubar et al.) and other 2020+ CMD codes (e.g. using randomized/Arnoldi eigensolvers) were not verified.

## Q2. Space-variant PSF / blur operator reduction (PSF interpolation, low-rank space-variant blur, product-convolution with error bounds; microscopy and astronomy)

### Takeaway
The dominant physics-based reduction for space-variant imaging operators is the **product-convolution (PC) / "PSF interpolation" model**: H x ≈ Σ_k h_k ∗ (w_k ⊙ x) (or the transpose order). Here h_k are a few PSFs (sampled or SVD/PCA "eigen-PSFs") and w_k are smooth weights, so the operator costs about K FFT convolutions. Nagy–O'Leary (piecewise interpolation), Flicker–Rigaut (PCA eigen-PSFs for AO), and Denis et al. 2015 (a unified matrix-decomposition framework plus an improved approximation) set the practice. Escande & Weiss supplied the theory: explicit approximation rates and complexity as functions of PSF-field smoothness, plus wavelet-domain sparsity. Alger et al. (2024) carried the same idea to matrix-free approximation of high-rank Hessians in PDE-constrained inverse problems.

### Cited Findings
- **Nagy & O'Leary (foundation):** the image is split into regions, each with a locally shift-invariant PSF, and the PSFs are linearly interpolated between regions. The operator becomes a sum of a few FFT convolutions of masked/weighted images, which gives a fast matrix-vector product for iterative restoration. J.G. Nagy & D.P. O'Leary, "Restoring images degraded by spatially variant blur," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 19(4), 1063–1082 (1998), doi:10.1137/S106482759528507X [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/S106482759528507X)
- **Flicker & Rigaut (astronomy/AO):** anisoplanatic AO PSFs are approximated by a truncated modal (PCA/SVD) expansion. The deconvolution operator becomes a sum of a few convolutions with eigen-PSFs, modulated by spatially varying coefficient maps. R.C. Flicker & F.J. Rigaut, "Anisoplanatic deconvolution of adaptive optics images," JOSA A 22(3), 504–513 (2005), doi:10.1364/JOSAA.22.000504 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.22.000504)
- **Denis, Thiébaut, Soulez, Becker & Mourya (2015):** a unified view of existing fast approximations (piecewise convolution, PSF interpolation, eigen-PSF/SVD) as matrix decompositions of the blur operator, plus a proposed general model with "low computational complexity while reducing approximation error". The abstract recommends this model for approximating *and inverting* a known shift-variant blur. "Fast approximations of shift-variant blur," Int. J. Comput. Vis. 115(3), 253–278 (2015), doi:10.1007/s11263-015-0817-x [V] — [HAL](https://hal-ujm.archives-ouvertes.fr/ujm-00979825); [PDF](https://hal-ujm.archives-ouvertes.fr/ujm-00979825v2/file/IJCV_denis_approx_inv_shiftvariant_blur_R2.pdf); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1007%2FS11263-015-0817-X)
- **Escande & Weiss, product-convolution theory:** for linear integral operators whose impulse responses vary regularly in time/space, they analyse PC expansions (local convolution approximation, FFT-based), design several types (piecewise-constant, piecewise-linear/spline interpolation, SVD-based), and give "explicit rates of approximation and their complexity, depending on the smoothness of the time-varying impulse response". P. Escande & P. Weiss, "Approximation of integral operators using product-convolution expansions," J. Math. Imaging Vis. 58, 333–348 (2017) [venue K]; arXiv:1604.04374 [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04374); [HAL](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01301235); [author PDF](https://pescande.perso.math.cnrs.fr/assets/publis/2016_Escande_Weiss_Approximation_of_Integral_Operators_using_Convolution-product_Expansions.pdf)
- **Escande & Weiss, PC to wavelet compression:** PC expansions can be used to build sparse wavelet-domain representations of space-varying operators quickly. Under their regularity assumption the algorithm reaches operator-norm error ≤ η with a complexity that is linear in the number of pixels m when α ≥ d (α = smoothness, d = dimension). "Fast wavelet decomposition of linear operators through product-convolution expansions," arXiv:2005.09870 (2020) [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09870); [HAL](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02612434); [PDF](https://www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/~weiss/Publis/Journals/2020/fastDecompOperators.pdf)
- Earlier companion: P. Escande & P. Weiss, "Sparse wavelet representations of spatially varying blurring operators," SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 8(4), 2976–3014 (2015), doi:10.1137/151003465 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/151003465)
- Follow-ups extend PC to blind/estimated settings (e.g. "Blind inverse problems with isolated spikes," arXiv:2111.02093) and compare "scattering vs gathering" forms of spatially varying blur, citing Escande et al. for PC error bounds ("Scattering and gathering for spatially varying blurs," arXiv:2303.05687). — [arXiv 2111.02093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02093); [arXiv 2303.05687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05687)
- **Hessian extension (matrix-free):** Alger, Hartland, Petra & Ghattas compute impulse responses of an operator at scattered points, interpolate them into kernel entries (a PC/"PSF" approximation for locally supported, nonnegative kernels), and compress the result as an H-matrix. A "moment method" gets many impulse responses (e.g. about 50) per operator application. The method approximates **high-rank** Hessians (where global low-rank fails) with few operator applications. "Point spread function approximation of high rank Hessians with locally supported non-negative integral kernels," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. (2024), doi:10.1137/23M1584745; arXiv:2307.03349 [V] — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/23M1584745); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03349)
- Applied to seismic inversion/UQ: "Accelerating seismic inversion and uncertainty quantification with efficient high-rank Hessian approximations," arXiv:2507.10804 (2025), which frames PC as a weighted sum of PSF convolutions with interpolation weights [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10804)

**Microscopy applications**
- Arigovindan et al. represent depth-varying widefield PSFs as a PCA-weighted sum, approximating the depth-variant response with a few depth-invariant convolutions ("parallel product-convolution"). This is more accurate than strata-based approximation. M. Arigovindan et al., "A parallel product-convolution approach for representing depth varying point spread functions in 3D widefield microscopy based on principal component analysis," Opt. - Patwary & Preza decompose depth-variant PSFs into an orthonormal (PCA) basis and cut the number of PSFs needed for the PCA. In their comparison this beat strata-based restoration on both accuracy and speed for thick samples. N. Patwary & C. Preza, Biomed. Opt. Express 6(10), 3826 (2015) [V venue/year; title K: "Image restoration for three-dimensional fluorescence microscopy using an orthonormal basis for efficient representation of depth-variant point-spread functions"] — [Memphis repository](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/13881)
- Yuan & Preza (SPIE 8227, 2012) found that reconstruction quality changes little with the number of PCA components beyond one, and suggested two components for experimental data. This is evidence that depth-variant PSF families are of very low rank. — [SPIE](https://ebooks.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/8227/82270H/Performance-evaluation-of-an-image-estimation-method-based-on-principal/10.1117/12.909638.full)
- **Miniscope3D (Yanny, Antipa, … Waller 2020):** GRIN-lens aberrations make the PSF field-varying. The forward model is a weighted sum of K shift-invariant kernels (low-rank PC). 64 calibration PSFs per depth predict more than 300,000 PSFs, the forward model needs about 10–20 convolutions, and resolution is about 2.2× better than with a shift-invariant model. K. Yanny et al., "Miniscope3D: optimized single-shot miniature 3D fluorescence microscopy," Light Sci. Appl. 9, 171 (2020), doi:10.1038/s41377-020-00403-7 [V venue; DOI K] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.05382); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7532148)

**Astronomy PSF fields (PSF interpolation as low-rank matrix factorization)**
- Euclid/LSST-type PSF field recovery uses low-rank factorization of the PSF stack with spatial-smoothness (graph-Laplacian) constraints, e.g. RCA ("Resolved Components Analysis"): F.M. Ngolè Mboula, J.-L. Starck, K. Okumura, J. Amiaux, P. Hudelot, "Constraint matrix factorization for space variant PSFs field restoration," Inverse Problems 32, 124001 (2016); M.A. Schmitz et al., "Euclid: Nonparametric point spread function field recovery through interpolation on a graph Laplacian," A&A 636, A78 (2020) [both K] — [A&A 2020 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201936094) (DOI from memory, unverified)

### Inferences
- **Accuracy/speed scaling:** PC with K kernels costs about K FFT pairs per application. Error falls at a rate set by the smoothness of the PSF field (Escande & Weiss). In practice K ≈ 2–20 in microscopy (Yuan & Preza: about 2; Miniscope3D: 10–20).
- **Direct lithography transfer:** (a) Field-dependent aberrations across a scanner slit (aberration varying with field position) are a space-variant imaging operator, and PC/eigen-PSF interpolation of the per-field-point SOCS kernels (interpolate kernel *sets* across field) is the natural analogue of Flicker–Rigaut/Nagy–O'Leary. (b) For ILT gradients, PC forms keep an exact adjoint, so the optimizer gets consistent gradients. (c) Alger et al.'s PSF/H-matrix Hessian approximation is a candidate for Newton/Gauss-Newton ILT, where the Hessian of the resist/aerial-image loss with respect to the mask is local (support about the optical radius λ/NA × a few) and nonnegative-kernel-like but high-rank. This matches the regime that method was built for.
- The Escande–Weiss rates suggest that smoothness of the TCC in focus or in Zernike coefficients (parameter space rather than image space) could also be exploited with interpolation-type (PC-in-parameter) expansions and provable error rates.

### Gaps
- Exact numerical speedups and error tables from Denis et al. 2015 and Escande & Weiss 2017 were not pulled (full texts not fetched).
- Volume/page/DOI for Escande & Weiss JMIV 2017 and for the RCA papers were not verified in this session.
- No paper was found that applies PC approximation explicitly to *partially coherent* (bilinear/TCC) space-variant imaging in microscopy. PC is mostly applied to linear, intensity-domain, incoherent PSFs.

## Q3. Low-rank or tensor approximations of propagation operators and aberration-parametrized imaging (Zernike-parametrized PSF expansions; extended Nijboer–Zernike theory)

### Takeaway
The extended Nijboer–Zernike (ENZ) theory of Janssen, Braat, Dirksen and van Haver is the main *analytic* reduced representation of aberrated, defocused PSFs. The pupil is expanded in Zernike circle polynomials, and each term's through-focus PSF contribution is a rapidly converging series of Bessel/power functions, available semi-analytically from low to high NA (scalar and vector). It turns the PSF into a linear (for small aberrations) or polynomial map in the Zernike coefficients with precomputable basis functions. ENZ originated at Philips/ASML-linked groups and was already used for lithographic lens characterization, so it transfers directly. Data-driven counterparts are PCA or Zernike-moment interpolation of depth-variant microscope PSFs.

### Cited Findings
- ENZ foundations: A.J.E.M. Janssen, "Extended Nijboer–Zernike approach for the computation of optical point-spread functions," JOSA A 19(5), 849–857 (2002), doi:10.1364/JOSAA.19.000849; J. Braat, P. Dirksen, A.J.E.M. Janssen, "Assessment of an extended Nijboer–Zernike approach for the computation of optical point-spread functions," JOSA A 19(5), 858–870 (2002), doi:10.1364/JOSAA.19.000858 [K] — [Janssen publication list (univie)](https://nuhagphp.univie.ac.at/janssen/data/p158.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.19.000849)

### Inferences
- **What ENZ gives as a ROM:** for aberration vector a = (a_nm), the pupil P = exp(i 2π Σ a_nm Z_nm) ≈ 1 + i2π Σ a_nm Z_nm + …. Each Zernike's focal-field contribution V_nm(r, φ; f) is known analytically as a function of defocus f. So the PSF over the whole (focus × aberration) process window is a *low-order polynomial* in a with precomputed analytic basis fields. This is an explicit separated (tensor-product) representation in (space) × (focus) × (aberration) coordinates.
- **Lithography transfer:** for Hopkins imaging, TCC(f, a) = ∫ J(s) P(s+f1; f, a) P*(s+f2; f, a) ds inherits this polynomial dependence. A Taylor/ENZ-expanded TCC (TCC_0 + Σ a_i TCC_i + Σ a_i a_j TCC_ij), or SOCS kernels computed in an ENZ basis, gives process-window-aware imaging and its derivatives (aberration sensitivity) without a new eigendecomposition per process condition. Lithography has used similar "Zernike-sensitivity TCC" expansions. ENZ adds analytic through-focus basis functions and convergence control, including at high NA in vector form.
- Microscopy evidence that 1–2 PCA components capture depth variation (Yuan & Preza) suggests the focus axis of the lithographic process window may also be compressible to a few modes per kernel. Hyper-NA EUV/DUV with strong aberrations will need more.

### Gaps
- No 2010+ paper was found that builds an explicit low-rank tensor (Tucker/TT) decomposition of an aberration-parametrized imaging operator in microscopy or astronomy. The tensor-format idea above is inferred.
- Quantitative ENZ convergence/speed numbers (terms needed versus aberration magnitude) were not extracted.

## Q4. Fast phase retrieval / inverse imaging with reduced models: Gauss-Newton and Hessian structure, randomized sketching, matrix-free low-rank operator approximation

### Takeaway
Second-order and Gauss-Newton phase retrieval have been shown to be robust in Fourier ptychography (Yeh et al. 2015, with diagonal Hessian approximations) and made matrix-free in ptychography (Levenberg–Marquardt with Jacobian-vector products, Kandel et al. 2021). Physics-based low-rank operator approximation without training data uses randomized range finding (Halko–Martinsson–Tropp) and Hessian low-rank or PSF/H-matrix approximations from PDE-constrained inverse problems (Bui-Thanh/Ghattas; Alger 2024). In imaging, the Gauss-Newton Hessian JᴴJ of an FFT-based forward model is a structured (block-Toeplitz/convolutional) operator, which makes PC/PSF-type Hessian approximations natural.

### Cited Findings
- **Yeh et al. (2015):** compared Fourier ptychography phase retrieval algorithms on experimental data. Choice of cost function matters most, and amplitude-based costs beat intensity-based ones because brightfield/darkfield intensities span a wide range. Noise, aberrations and mis-calibration are the main error sources. They proposed a robust global Newton-type method, and the sequential Gauss-Newton version keeps only diagonal Hessian terms plus a constant regularizer for tractable inversion. L.-H. Yeh, J. Dong, J. Zhong, L. Tian, M. Chen, G. Tang, M. Soltanolkotabi, L. Waller, "Experimental robustness of Fourier ptychography phase retrieval algorithms," Opt. Express 23(26), 33214–33240 (2015), doi:10.1364/OE.23.033214 [V title/venue; DOI K] — [arXiv 1511.02986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.02986); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/282158xq)
- **Matrix-free Levenberg–Marquardt for ptychography:** uses Jacobian-vector and Hessian-vector products (no explicit Hessian), with a generalized-Gauss-Newton PSD approximation to keep descent on the nonconvex objective. "A matrix-free Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm for efficient ptychographic phase retrieval," arXiv:2103.01767 (2021) [V]; published as S. Kandel, S. Maddali, Y.S.G. Nashed, S.O. Hruszkewycz, C. Jacobsen, M. Allain, "Efficient ptychographic phase retrieval via a matrix-free Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm," Opt. Express 29(15), 23019 (2021) [K] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01767)
- **Randomized SVD (operator-agnostic, matrix-free):** randomized range finding needs only O(k+p) applications of A and Aᴴ to get a rank-k approximation, with probabilistic error bounds. N. Halko, P.G. Martinsson, J.A. Tropp, "Finding structure with randomness: probabilistic algorithms for constructing approximate matrix decompositions," SIAM Review 53(2), 217–288 (2011), doi:10.1137/090771806 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/090771806)
- **Low-rank Hessian in large-scale inverse problems:** data-misfit Hessians that are compact (rapid spectral decay) are approximated by randomized/Lanczos low-rank updates for Newton-CG and Laplace-approximation UQ. T. Bui-Thanh, O. Ghattas, J. Martin, G. Stadler, "A computational framework for infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems Part I," SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 35(6), A2494–A2523 (2013), doi:10.1137/12089586X [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/12089586X)
- **When the Hessian is high-rank:** global low-rank fails for local, high-rank Hessians, such as those in high-resolution imaging. The PSF/product-convolution + H-matrix approximation (Alger et al. 2024) builds a usable approximation from few operator applications (about 50 impulse responses per application via moment method). — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/23M1584745); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03349)
- **Low-rank/partially coherent inverse problems in microscopy:** mixed-state ptychography (Thibault & Menzel 2013, [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nature11806)) and compressive mutual-intensity recovery (Tian et al. 2012, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.1322)) put the low-rank structure into the *unknown*. That keeps per-iteration cost at about (number of modes) × (coherent forward cost).

### Inferences
- **ILT transfer:** ILT is structurally a partially coherent phase/amplitude retrieval problem with a binary/greyscale mask unknown. (a) Amplitude-type losses (√I) improve robustness when intensities span a wide dynamic range (Yeh 2015). This is worth testing in ILT losses, where contour regions are near threshold and bright/dark areas dominate L2-intensity losses. (b) Matrix-free Gauss-Newton/LM with SOCS-based Jacobian-vector products costs about 2× a gradient evaluation per inner CG step. (c) The Gauss-Newton Hessian of a SOCS aerial image with respect to the mask is Σ_k (H_k-weighted) convolution-type operators modulated by the current field, i.e. a PC-structured operator, so Alger-type PSF Hessian approximations or diagonal + low-rank preconditioners fit it.
- Randomized SVD of the Hopkins bilinear operator (applying the TCC to random pupil-plane vectors without forming the 4D TCC) is a matrix-free route to SOCS kernels. Its cost scales with the number of source points × kernel count rather than with TCC size. This is an inference supported by Halko et al.; no imaging-specific paper was verified.

### Gaps
- No verified computational-imaging paper uses *randomized sketching* (e.g. sketched Gauss-Newton or subsampled Hessians) specifically for phase retrieval with reported speedups. The literature exists (stochastic/minibatch ptychography is common) but was not checked here.
- No quantitative Gauss-Newton vs. first-order speedup numbers were extracted from Yeh 2015 or Kandel 2021.

## Q5. Hierarchical / multiresolution optics simulation: Gaussian beam decomposition, Gabor frames, fast Fresnel/angular-spectrum methods with sampling-aware reductions, scaled/shifted FFT

### Takeaway
On the propagation side, physics-based reduction mostly means *sampling-aware* angular-spectrum (AS) and Fresnel methods. Band-limited AS (Matsushima & Shimobaba 2009) removes aliasing by cutting off the transfer function. Shifted AS (2010) and scaled AS (2012) handle off-axis or zoomed windows. Adaptive-sampling AS (2020) uses the full space-bandwidth product. Scalable AS (Heintzmann et al., Optica 2023) zooms at about the cost of 3 FFTs and remains valid at high NA. 2026 electron-microscopy work reports grid reductions of about 10⁶. Gaussian beamlet decomposition (GBD, Worku & Gross and later) is the main ray-wave hybrid for non-paraxial, multi-surface systems.

### Cited Findings
**Angular spectrum family**
- **Band-limited AS:** zeroing the AS transfer function above a distance-dependent cutoff avoids transfer-function undersampling (aliasing) without enlarging the grid. Its weakness is that at long distances the number of valid frequency samples shrinks and accuracy drops. K. Matsushima & T. Shimobaba, "Band-limited angular spectrum method for numerical simulation of free-space propagation in far and near fields," Opt. Express 17(22), 19662–19673 (2009), doi:10.1364/OE.17.019662 [K biblio; limitation V via] — [arXiv 2203.10966 discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10966)
- **Shifted AS:** K. Matsushima, "Shifted angular spectrum method for off-axis numerical propagation," Opt. Express 18(17), 18453–18463 (2010), doi:10.1364/OE.18.018453 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.18.018453)
- **Scaled AS:** T. Shimobaba, K. Matsushima, T. Kakue, N. Masuda, T. Ito, "Scaled angular spectrum method," Opt. Lett. 37(19), 4128–4130 (2012), doi:10.1364/OL.37.004128 [K] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.37.004128)
- **Adaptive-sampling AS:** W. Zhang, H. Zhang, G. Jin, "Adaptive-sampling angular spectrum method with full utilization of space-bandwidth product," Opt. Lett. 45(16), 4416–4419 (2020) [K]; cited as a long-distance extension in — [arXiv 2203.10966](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.10966)
- **Controllable-energy AS (2022):** benchmarked against band-limited, wide-window, wide-range and adaptive-sampling variants. "Controllable energy angular spectrum method," arXiv:2203.10966 [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10966)
- **Modified scaling AS:** a scaling factor allows free choice of input-plane sampling, which shrinks the calculation window for long-distance propagation. Chinese Physics B (2021), doi:10.1088/1674-1056/abd38d [V] — [CPB](https://cpb.iphy.ac.cn/EN/10.1088/1674-1056/abd38d)
- **Scalable AS (SAS):** R. Heintzmann, L. Loetgering, F. Wechsler, "Scalable angular spectrum propagation," Optica 10(11), 1407 (2023) [V venue; vol/pages K]. Allows a destination pixel pitch larger than the source pitch (zoom), costs roughly 3 FFTs, and stays valid at high NA. — [EPFL Infoscience](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/308314); [Optica Open preprint](https://preprints.opticaopen.org/articles/preprint/Scalable_angular_spectrum_propagation/23498453)
- **2026 electron-optics follow-up:** Nekula, Bělín & Konečná propose a scaling AS method (SASM) and a "no-lensing" AS variant (NLASM). A scaling factor of 1000 cuts grid points by about 10⁶. "Efficient methods for wave propagation in electron microscopy," arXiv:2603.16607 (2026) [V] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16607)
- **2026 review:** a J. Beijing Inst. Technol. review covers band-limited and scalable AS methods and notes that flexible-geometry methods lose physical accuracy in extreme near-field and wide-angle regimes. doi:10.15918/j.jbit1004-0579.2026.042 [V] — [JBIT](https://journal.hep.com.cn/jbit/EN/10.15918/j.jbit1004-0579.2026.042)

**Gaussian beam / beamlet decomposition**
- GBD approximates a field as a finite set of coherent Gaussian beams, each tracked along a ray with a complex beam parameter (waist and curvature), which allows non-paraxial, multi-surface propagation. Worku & Gross extended the basis to curved, truncated, polarized and chirped beamlets for sharp apertures and high-NA systems, and applied Gaussian pulsed beam decomposition to ultrashort pulses (JOSA A, 2020). — [arXiv 2404.12454, "A generalized expression for accelerating beamlet decomposition simulations"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.12454); [X-MOL record of Worku & Gross JOSA A 2020](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/6026072); [N.G. Worku dissertation, Univ. Jena](https://db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00052572/dissworku.pdf)
- GBD hybridized with Fourier propagation for observatory/coronagraph modelling: "Hybrid propagation physics for the design and modeling of astronomical observatories: a coronagraphic example," arXiv:2310.20026 (2023) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.20026); comparison of GBD with FFT/mode-expansion methods for precision interferometry: [PMC10674269 / arXiv 2210.06905](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10674269/)
- GBD is implemented in commercial (CODE V, FRED, ASAP) and open-source tools. — [Raypier docs](https://raypier-optics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gaussian_beamlet_propagation.html)

### Inferences
- **Lithography transfer:** (a) Thin-mask Hopkins imaging needs only pupil-plane FFTs, but rigorous/3D-mask and through-focus or through-resist simulations need near-field propagation. Band-limited and scaled AS keep these alias-free on small grids, and SAS-type zoom lets the mask grid (sub-nm for 3D EMF) and the image/resist grid differ without padding. (b) Shifted AS fits off-axis source points in Abbe imaging: each tilted plane wave can be handled with a shifted window instead of a larger global grid. (c) GBD is less relevant for the projection optics, where pupil-based Hopkins already acts as the reduced model, but it is relevant to illuminator/freeform-source modelling.
- Sampling-aware methods reduce *grid size* (a constant-factor or space-bandwidth reduction). They are complementary to the *rank* reductions in Q1–Q3 and can be stacked: each SOCS/coherent-mode propagation can use a band-limited/scaled AS.

### Gaps
- No 2010+ paper was found that uses Gabor frames explicitly for optical wave-propagation ROM in this session's searches (the search returned no Gabor-frame hits). This remains unverified rather than disproved.
- No hierarchical/multiresolution (wavelet/multigrid) optical propagator with verified speedups was found within the tool budget.

## Q6. Synthesis: ideas most transferable to lithographic imaging and ILT, method comparison table, and ML delimitation

### Takeaway
The most transferable physics-based ideas are: (1) **parameter-space reduction of the TCC/SOCS kernels over the process window** using ENZ/Zernike analytic expansions plus PC-style interpolation with Escande–Weiss rates; (2) **product-convolution / eigen-PSF interpolation for field-dependent aberrations** across the slit; (3) **matrix-free kernel and Hessian construction** (randomized SVD of the Hopkins operator; Alger-type PSF/H-matrix Gauss-Newton Hessians for ILT); (4) **separable/low-tensor-rank coherent-mode computation** as in ESRF 1D-CMD; and (5) **sampling-aware propagators** (band-limited/scaled/scalable AS) for the rigorous-mask and resist parts.

**ML delimitation (out of scope):** learned surrogates for space-variant deconvolution, e.g. MultiWienerNet (multiple differentiable Wiener filters + CNN; reported 625–1600× faster than iterative shift-varying methods; Yanny, Monakhova, Shuai, Waller, Optica 2022), sit outside physics-based ROM. They are noted only to mark the boundary. — [Waller lab publications](https://www.laurawaller.com/publications/?tgid=1). Data-calibrated *physics-structured* models such as Miniscope3D's PC model or Debarnot–Escande–Mangeat–Weiss "Learning low-dimensional models of microscopes" (IEEE TCI 2021) [K] count as in scope, because the operator structure (PC/low-rank) is physical and only the coefficients are fitted.

### Inferences
- **Priority ranking for lithography/ILT (inference):**
  1. *ENZ/Zernike-polynomial TCC or SOCS expansion over focus and aberrations.* Analytic, has a lithography pedigree (Dirksen/Braat/Janssen at Philips Research), and directly enables process-window-aware ILT with cheap derivatives.
  2. *PC/eigen-kernel interpolation across field position and across process parameters, with Escande–Weiss rates as a guide for knot spacing.* Enables full-slit, field-dependent-aberration OPC/ILT at about K× the cost of a single field point.
  3. *Matrix-free Gauss-Newton ILT* with SOCS Jacobian-vector products and PSF/H-matrix or diagonal+low-rank Hessian preconditioning (Yeh 2015 → Kandel 2021 → Alger 2024).
  4. *Randomized/separable CMD to build SOCS kernels* without forming the 4D TCC (Halko 2011; ESRF 1D-CMD separability).
  5. *Sampling-aware AS* for 3D-mask/resist modules and multi-scale grids.
- **Main caveat:** most imaging-side PC/PCA reductions act on *linear incoherent* PSFs. Lithographic imaging is *bilinear* (TCC), so reductions must be applied to the kernel set (or to the pupil/source factors) rather than to the intensity PSF. CMD/SOCS and ENZ-in-pupil do this natively, while PC must be applied per SOCS kernel or to the pupil function.

### Gaps
- No verified paper combines CMD with parameter-space (focus/aberration) interpolation of the modes in X-ray or microscopy optics. This is the most lithography-relevant missing piece, and its value remains an inference.
- Quantitative cross-domain benchmarks (e.g. error vs. number of modes for a lithography-like annular source) are not available from the sources checked.
- Several bibliographic details marked [K] (pages/DOIs for Nagy–O'Leary, Flicker–Rigaut, Escande–Weiss JMIV, ENZ 2002 papers, Matsushima 2009/2010, Shimobaba 2012, Zhang 2020, Halko 2011, Bui-Thanh 2013, Lindberg & Kim 2015, Zhong 2016, Maalouf 2011, Debarnot 2021, RCA papers) come from prior knowledge and were not re-fetched, because the arxiv.org and journals.iucr.org fetches failed from this environment.
